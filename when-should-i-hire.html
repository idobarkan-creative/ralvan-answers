When Should I Make My First Hire?

Feeling overwhelmed is not a hiring signal. A measurable constraint that costs more than the salary is.

The usual trigger for a first hire is exhaustion. That is understandable and it is a poor

signal, because it tells you that you are busy without telling you what you are busy with.

Plenty of overwhelmed owners hire and stay overwhelmed, having added management overhead to the

original problem.

The test that works

Hire when a specific, named constraint is demonstrably costing you more than the fully loaded

cost of the person who would remove it.

Three parts, all required. Specific, meaning you can describe the work in a

sentence. Costing you, meaning you can point at revenue lost or opportunity

foregone. More than the salary, including payroll costs, tools, and the

management time you will spend, which owners routinely underestimate.

If you cannot complete that sentence, you have a prioritisation problem rather than a capacity

problem, and hiring will not fix it.

Find out where the time actually goes first

Log your time for one week in rough blocks. Nearly every owner is surprised, and the surprise

usually falls into one of three patterns.

  • Most hours in low value repetitive work. The clearest case for hiring, and

often the case for automating instead.

  • Most hours in work only you can do. Hiring will not help directly. Something

must be removed from your plate elsewhere to create room.

  • Hours scattered with no pattern. This is a focus problem. A hire will

inherit the chaos and amplify it.

What to hire first

The instinct is to hire someone like yourself, which feels comfortable and rarely helps.

Better options, roughly in order of how often they are correct:

  • The thing you are worst at that the business needs constantly. Usually

bookkeeping, operations, or fulfilment. Unglamorous and high return.

  • The thing that generates revenue that you personally bottleneck. If you are

the only person who can sell, that ceiling is your revenue ceiling.

  • The thing you hate. Not a joke. Work you avoid gets done late and badly,

and the cost of that is real but invisible.

Hire last for work that is strategic, judgment heavy, or defines the character of the

business. That work is genuinely hard to delegate early and delegating it badly is expensive.

Contractor before employee, usually

For a first hire, a contractor or part time arrangement is often the better structure. You

learn whether the role is real, you learn whether you can manage someone, and you can reverse the

decision cheaply if you were wrong about either. Many owners discover that the constraint they

identified was not the actual constraint, and finding that out at contractor cost is

considerably better than finding it out at employment cost.

The question underneath

Before hiring, it is worth asking whether you want a bigger business or a better one. These

diverge more often than people expect. A first hire moves you toward the first, and if what you

actually wanted was the second, pricing, focus, and dropping unprofitable work get you there

without adding a person.
