AI Business Advisors Compared: What Each Format Does

Several different products get called AI business advisors. They solve different problems, and picking by name rather than by format leads people wrong.

The label covers at least four distinct formats. They fail in different ways, so the useful

comparison is structural rather than brand by brand.

Format 1: the general assistant

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and similar. Extremely capable, broad, effectively free.

Good for: a specific question with a knowable answer. Drafting. Explaining

something unfamiliar. Working through a calculation.

Fails at: anything requiring continuity. It does not remember your business

between sessions, so advice never compounds and you re-explain context every time. It also

converges toward the reasonable middle, which is exactly wrong for decisions where the whole

question is which extreme to accept.

Format 2: the AI coach

Products built around a coaching methodology, often with goal tracking and check ins.

Good for: accountability and habit. Regular prompting to review what you

said you would do.

Fails at: hard analytical calls. Coaching frameworks are built to draw

answers out of you, which assumes you have the answer. Sometimes you do not, and being asked

what you think about it repeatedly is not useful.

Format 3: the specialist tool

Narrow products for pricing, financial modelling, competitive research, or marketing

analysis.

Good for: depth in one domain. Better than any generalist inside its

boundary.

Fails at: the cross functional question, which is what most real decisions

are. Whether to open a second location is simultaneously financial, operational, and personal.

Every specialist answers its own slice confidently and none owns the whole.

Format 4: the AI board

Several persistent advisors with conflicting frameworks, meeting on a schedule, ending in a

recorded decision.

Good for: consequential decisions with real tradeoffs, where you need to see

the strongest version of each side rather than a blended recommendation. Also for the ongoing

accountability of a fixed cadence.

Fails at: speed. The format is overhead for a question with a knowable

answer. It also cannot supply what makes real boards powerful: genuine accountability, a

network, and people with a stake in the outcome.

Choosing by question, not by product

What you are facingFormat that fits
A factual or technical questionGeneral assistant
You know what to do and are not doing itAI coach, or a peer group
A deep question inside one domainSpecialist tool
A tradeoff with no clean answerAI board
You need introductions or capitalNone of these. You need people.

What no AI format currently provides

Worth being direct about the ceiling. None of these has a stake in your outcome, can make an

introduction, can invest, or can remove you when you are the problem. Those are the things real

advisors provide and no current product substitutes for them.

What AI formats do provide is availability, patience, and a willingness to argue at eleven at

night about a decision you cannot discuss with your team. For a large number of business owners

who have no board and never will, that gap is worth closing even though it is not the same

thing.
